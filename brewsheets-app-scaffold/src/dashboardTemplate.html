What this number includes. Materials only: beer at its Unleashed cost per litre, plus kegs, caps, collars and other packaging. Labour, overheads, energy and excise are not in it, so treat it as materials landed cost, not full cost of goods. Kegs with no size in their Unleashed name are treated as 50L.

Efficiency summary

How to read this. Each finished beer assembly is one batch, matched to its kegs and cubes through the Unleashed batch number and the beer (the batch and beer the keg or cube line actually drew from, so two beers that share a batch number stay separate batches). Transfer efficiency is litres in the completed assembly divided by the litres assumed to go into the fermenter, which is the completed volume rounded up to the next 2,500 L unless the real figure is available: litres typed into the table below come first, then litres loaded from the Brewery Efficiency Tracking sheet (dashed cells), then the rounded-up assumption. Brite to packaged is nominal litres packaged (kegs at their size, cubes at 6 L) divided by litres in the assembly. Overall is litres packaged divided by the assumed fermenter volume. Monthly figures are weighted by litres, not averages of percentages. Small brews (under 1,500 L) are left out, and batches still being packaged are left out of packaging and overall figures.

Volume summary

How to read this. Volumes are completed assemblies in Unleashed. Finished beer is counted in the month its assembly was completed, which can be weeks after the brew date; kegs and cubes by their production date. Parked assemblies (not yet completed) are not counted. Beer is assigned to a range by the SKUs that use it most. Kegs follow the keg size filter, which defaults to 50L. Litres for kegs and cubes are the beer actually used, not including loss.
